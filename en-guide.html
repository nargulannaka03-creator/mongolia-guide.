01

What locals really do

Everyday tricks Mongolians take for granted

TAXI · GETTING AROUND

Hold out your hand and an ordinary car stops

In Ulaanbaatar, if you stand at the roadside with your arm out low, a private car, not a taxi, may stop and offer you a ride. For locals this is completely normal. Always agree on the destination and price before you get in.

Visitors are safer with a ride-hailing app.

TRAFFIC · JAMS

At rush hour, walking is faster

Traffic in the capital is among the worst anywhere. Locals know that for a few kilometers, walking wins. “Traffic” is an excuse everyone accepts for being late.

Leave extra time, and extra patience.

MONEY · PAYING

QR codes in the city, cash in the countryside

Cards and QR payments work almost everywhere in Ulaanbaatar. Out on the steppe there is often no signal and no ATM. Carry plenty of small notes.

SHOPPING · BARGAINING

Haggle at markets, and only at markets

At open-air markets like Narantuul, bargaining is expected. Never haggle in department stores or chains. For cashmere, factory outlet stores beat tourist souvenir shops on both quality and price.

FOOD · EATING OUT

The best places have the smallest signs

Locals eat at simple canteens called guanz. The lunch set (Ланч) is cheap and huge. For real buuz and khuushuur, skip the tourist restaurants and come here.

STEPPE · VISITING HERDERS

Bring sweets as a gift

On the steppe, even a stranger passing by is welcomed with tea. The best thank-you gifts are things only sold in town: candy, cookies, fruit. Families with children love them most.

02

Classic traveler mistakes

What went wrong, and what to do instead

My phone was stolen at the market

Crowded markets and packed buses are pickpocket hotspots.

DO THIS INSTEAD

Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or a bag in front of you. Wear your backpack on your chest. Locals do the same.

Too much airag, and an upset stomach

Airag (айраг), fermented mare's milk, is the taste of summer, and hosts keep offering it.

DO THIS INSTEAD

Go slowly the first time. Even Mongolians who aren't used to it get stomach trouble. Saying “just a little” is perfectly fine.

I asked where the toilet was, and they pointed at the steppe

In the countryside a toilet may be a pit with walls, or there may be none at all.

DO THIS INSTEAD

Always carry toilet paper and wet wipes. Herders say “I'm going to check on the horses” when they mean the toilet.

I went in October in summer clothes and froze

The steppe swings hard between day and night. Even in autumn, mornings drop below zero.

DO THIS INSTEAD

Pack a light down jacket and a hat in any season. Mongolians themselves say you get four seasons in one day. It can even snow in summer.

The bumpy roads made me too sick to enjoy the view

Outside the city most roads are unpaved, and drives last hours.

DO THIS INSTEAD

Bring motion-sickness pills. Locals jokingly call the shaking a “Mongolian massage”.

03

Real life in Mongolia

What you only learn by living there

WINTER · THE COLDEST CAPITAL

How Ulaanbaatar's winter feels

0°CStill autumn. One jacket
−10°CNo gloves? Painful
−20°CEyelashes start to freeze
−30°CPhone battery suddenly dies
−40°CJanuary mornings get this cold

On some winter days, smoke from heating makes the air bad. Locals wear masks. Keep your phone in an inside pocket so your body heat keeps it alive.

FAMILY

Cousins are basically siblings

Family ties are very close, and cousins are often called brothers and sisters. People seriously say that everyone in the country is connected through someone. That's life in a nation of about 3.5 million.

TIME

Plans are “roughly”

Weather, roads and livestock can change any plan, and everyone accepts that. “I'll come tomorrow” can mean three days later. Waiting calmly is the steppe way.

YOUTH CULTURE

Korean convenience stores, Mongolian rap

Korean-style convenience stores are on every corner in Ulaanbaatar, and young people love K-pop and Mongolian hip-hop. Some bands mix throat singing and the horsehead fiddle with rap. The steppe and the city live side by side.

FOOD

Meat is the star. Vegetables are extras

Traditional meals center on mutton and dairy. Vegetarians struggle in the countryside. The capital has more and more vegetarian restaurants, so eat well there first.
