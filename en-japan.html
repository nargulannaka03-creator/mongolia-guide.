01 Culture

Mongolian culture: the basics

The foundations of life on the steppe.

Nomads and the five animals

Таван хошуу мал

Herders live with the five animals, horses, cattle, camels, sheep and goats, and move to new pastures with the seasons. Many nomadic families still live in the countryside.

The ger

Гэр

A round home of felt stretched over a wooden frame. It can be put up or taken down in a few hours, so the whole house moves with the family. The capital has ger districts too.

White food, red food

Цагаан идээ · Улаан идээ

Summer is the season of dairy, the “white food”. Winter is the season of meat, the “red food”. Salty milk tea is part of every day.

Two scripts

Кирилл · Монгол бичиг

Everyday writing uses the Cyrillic alphabet, but the vertical traditional Mongolian script is also taught in schools and is increasingly used in official settings.

Horsehead fiddle and throat singing

Морин хуур · Хөөмий

The morin khuur, a fiddle topped with a carved horse's head, and khöömii, where one singer produces two notes at once, are both UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Faith

Бурхан шашин · Бөө мөргөл

Tibetan Buddhism is widely practiced, and shamanic respect for the Eternal Blue Sky (Tengri) and nature remains part of daily life.

02 Compare

What we share, and what we don't

Japan on the left, Mongolia on the right. Switch with the tabs.

The “Mongolian spot”
JAPAN

Many babies are born with a blue birthmark on the lower back that fades as they grow.

MONGOLIA

The mark is named after Mongolia, and almost every Mongolian baby has one.

Word order
JAPAN

Watashi wa gohan wo taberu (I rice eat). The verb comes last.

MONGOLIA

Би хоол идэнэ (I food eat). Same order, with endings that work like Japanese particles.

Sumo
JAPAN

Sumo is the national sport, and several grand champions (yokozuna) have come from Mongolia.

MONGOLIA

Traditional wrestling, Бөх (bökh), is the highlight of Naadam. Children grow up with it.

Zodiac and the lunar calendar
JAPAN

People draw the year's zodiac animal on New Year cards.

MONGOLIA

The same twelve animals are used, and New Year is still celebrated by the lunar calendar as Tsagaan Sar.

Respect for elders
JAPAN

People use honorific speech and offer elders the seat of honor.

MONGOLIA

Elders sit at the back of the ger, and at New Year the young support their elders' arms in greeting.

A textbook story
JAPAN

Many people read “Suho's White Horse” in elementary school.

MONGOLIA

It is based on a Mongolian folk tale about how the horsehead fiddle began.

Sumo rules
JAPAN

Wrestlers fight in a ring. You lose if you step out or touch the ground with anything but your feet.

MONGOLIA

No ring and no time limit. You lose when your elbow, knee or back touches the ground.

Names
JAPAN

A family shares one surname.

MONGOLIA

People normally go by “father's name + own name”. Clan names, similar to surnames, were revived from the 1990s.

Homes
JAPAN

People build a house in one place and stay.

MONGOLIA

Nomads move their homes. Cities mix apartment blocks with ger districts.

Food
JAPAN

Fish and rice. Raw fish is common.

MONGOLIA

Mutton and dairy. A landlocked country, so fish is eaten less.

Tea
JAPAN

Green tea, drunk plain.

MONGOLIA

Tea boiled with milk and a pinch of salt.

Climate
JAPAN

Humid, with a rainy season.

MONGOLIA

Dry and continental. Ulaanbaatar is called the coldest capital in the world, with winters below −30°C.

03 History

A timeline of Mongolian history

Red marks show events connected to Japan.

Mongolian historyLinks with Japan
  1. 3rd c. BC

    The Xiongnu build a state on the steppe

    The first great nomadic empire rises on the Mongolian Plateau.

  2. 1206

    Genghis Khan founds the Mongol Empire

    He unites the steppe tribes. The empire grows into one of the largest land empires in history.

  3. 1271

    Kublai Khan proclaims the Yuan dynasty

    Genghis's grandson rules China from Dadu, today's Beijing.

  4. 1274·81

    The Mongol invasions of JapanJAPAN

    Yuan forces attack Kyushu twice. This is the first major contact between Japan and Mongolia.

  5. 1368

    The Yuan retreat from China to the steppe

    Mongolia then splits into rival groups.

  6. 17th c.

    Mongolia comes under Qing rule

    The Qing dynasty governs the region for about 200 years.

  7. 1911

    Independence from the Qing is declared

    As the Qing collapses, Mongolia declares independence under the Bogd Khan.

  8. 1924

    The Mongolian People's Republic is founded

    It becomes the world's second socialist state, after the Soviet Union.

  9. 1939

    Battles of Khalkhin Gol (Nomonhan)JAPAN

    Japanese and Manchukuo forces fight Soviet and Mongolian forces over the border.

  10. 1972

    Japan and Mongolia establish diplomatic relationsJAPAN

    Economic cooperation and people-to-people exchange begin in earnest.

  11. 1990

    Democracy and a market economy

    A peaceful democratic movement brings multi-party politics.

  12. 1992

    A new constitution; the country becomes “Mongolia”

    Genghis Khan is honored once again as a national hero.

  13. 1990s–

    Mongolian wrestlers shine in Japanese sumoJAPAN

    The first Mongolian wrestlers joined in 1992. Several have since become yokozuna, linking the two countries.
