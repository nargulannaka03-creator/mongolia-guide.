Before you step
over the threshold

In Mongolia, respect for fire, water, the sky and elders lives on in everyday gestures. Here are the manners every visitor should know, and the things you should never do.

Four things to remember first

ClockwiseIn a ger or a temple, move left to right, following the sun
Right handGive and take with your right hand. Support your right elbow with your left to be extra polite
Fire and waterThe hearth and running water are sacred. Never dirty them or step over them
EldersOffer them the seat and the food first, and don't interrupt them

Visiting a ger

Гэрт зочлох
  • DO

    Call out “Nokhoi khorio!” from outside

    Herders keep guard dogs. Shout Нохой хорио! (“Hold the dog!”) and wait for someone to come out.

  • TABOO

    Stepping or sitting on the threshold

    The threshold (босго) guards the family's luck. Stepping on it tramples their good fortune. Step over it.

  • DO

    Go left once inside, and move clockwise

    By tradition, men sit on the west side (left as you enter) and women on the east (right).

  • TABOO

    Taking the seat of honor uninvited

    The back of the ger (хоймор) holds the family altar and heirlooms. It is the seat for elders and honored guests.

  • TABOO

    Walking between the two pillars or leaning on them

    The pillars (багана) hold up the roof and link the home to the sky. Don't pass between them or lean on them.

  • TABOO

    Entering with rolled-up sleeves

    It looks like you're ready for a fight. Roll your sleeves down before you go in.

Fire and water are sacred

Гал ба ус
  • TABOO

    Throwing trash into the fire or pouring water on it

    A fire spirit is believed to live in the hearth and protect the family. Dirtying it insults the home itself.

  • TABOO

    Pointing your feet at the fire or stepping over it

    When you sit, keep your feet from pointing toward the hearth.

  • TABOO

    Pointing a blade at the fire

    Poking the fire with a knife, or turning a blade toward it, is said to wound the fire spirit.

  • TABOO

    Polluting rivers and springs

    Washing clothes, urinating or dumping trash in running water is a serious wrong. On the steppe, water is life.

Accepting hospitality

Зочлох ёс
  • DO

    Accept with your right hand or both hands

    Take tea (сүүтэй цай) and food with your right hand. To show extra respect, support your right elbow with your left hand.

  • DO

    Taste a little of everything you're offered

    Refusing is rude. You don't have to finish it, just take a sip or a bite before setting it down.

  • DO

    Flick vodka three times with your ring finger

    An offering to the sky, the earth and the ancestors. If you don't drink, touch the glass to your lips and hand it back. That's perfectly polite.

  • TABOO

    Giving or taking with only your left hand

    Using just the left hand comes across as disrespectful.

Gestures and the body

Биеийн хэлэмж
  • DO

    Shake hands if your feet touch

    If you accidentally step on or kick someone's foot, shake hands right away. Leaving it is said to invite a quarrel.

  • TABOO

    Touching someone's hat or putting it on the floor

    A hat is sacred, like the head. Don't step on it or over it either.

  • TABOO

    Touching someone's head

    The head is seen as the seat of the soul, not something to touch casually.

  • TABOO

    Pointing at people with one finger

    To show a direction or a person, use your whole hand, palm up.

  • DO

    Hand over a knife handle first

    Pointing the blade at someone signals hostility.

At sacred places

Овоо ба хадаг
  • DO

    Walk around an ovoo three times clockwise and add a stone

    A custom to pray for a safe journey. Drivers passing by sometimes honk in greeting.

  • DO

    Offer a khadag with both hands

    The blue silk scarf (хадаг) is a gift of respect in the color of the sky. Present it with the fold facing the receiver.

  • DO

    Clockwise at temples and prayer wheels too

    Walk around stupas and spin prayer wheels in the direction of the sun.

Lunar New Year: Tsagaan Sar

Цагаан сар
  • DO

    Greet elders by supporting their arms from below

    In the New Year greeting золгох, the younger person holds out both arms under the elder's arms and they touch cheeks. It shows respect by literally supporting the elder.

  • DO

    Celebrate with white foods

    “Tsagaan Sar” means “White Month”, and dairy stands for purity. Visitors are welcomed with dairy foods and buuz, steamed dumplings.

Careful in conversation

Яриа
  • TABOO

    Talking as if Mongolia were part of China or Russia

    Mongolians are proud of their independence. Keep Mongolia distinct from Inner Mongolia, an autonomous region of China.

  • TABOO

    Making light of Genghis Khan

    He is deeply honored as the founder of the nation. Don't make him the butt of a joke.

  • DO

    Put elders first

    Don't interrupt them, and offer seats and food to the eldest first. People often add a respectful term after an elder's name.

True or false challenge

Try it at the booth. In a Mongolian home, is this OK?

Customs vary a little by region and family. When in doubt, follow what the people around you do.
